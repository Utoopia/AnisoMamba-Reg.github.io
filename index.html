Overview

Aligning the end-diastolic (ED) and end-systolic (ES) frames of a cardiac cine MRI is a basic step for estimating myocardial strain, checking wall motion, and measuring cardiac function. This thesis presents AnisoMamba-Reg, an unsupervised deformable registration network built on Mamba, a selective state-space model that reads 3D features as a 1D sequence.

The order in which that sequence is read decides which voxels end up as neighbors. Cardiac MRI is strongly anisotropic (1.8 × 1.8 × 10.0 mm), so we redesign the scan order around that fact instead of treating all three axes alike.

Contributions

  • An anisotropic patch embedding and a Siamese Mamba encoder that combines the two images' features by sum and difference.
  • A scan ordering that reads in-plane (H, W) tokens first: HWD and WHD permutations, each traversed forward and in reverse (four paths, called Flip4-scan).
  • A Laplacian-pyramid decoder with feature warping (adopted from LapWarp), combined with these encoder features, followed by velocity-field integration.

Motivation

Why ED–ES registration matters

The deformation field from ED to ES is the starting point for strain estimation, regional wall-motion analysis, and other measures of cardiac function [1, 2]. Errors in the field carry over into these measurements, for example when judging functional recovery after myocardial infarction or telling apart conditions such as hypertrophic cardiomyopathy.

The task is demanding. ED and ES differ by a large deformation, contraction (ED→ES) and relaxation (ES→ED) are not equally hard, and the network has to capture both the global change of shape and local changes at tissue boundaries.

Mamba and the order of the sequence

CNN-based registration has a limited receptive field, and Transformer-based registration becomes expensive as the number of tokens grows. Mamba models long-range dependencies efficiently, but it processes a 1D sequence. A 3D feature map therefore has to be flattened, and the flattening order determines which voxels are neighbors in the sequence.

Cardiac MRI is anisotropic

Cine MRI is acquired as thick short-axis slices. After resampling, the in-plane spacing is 1.8 mm and the slice spacing is 10.0 mm, about 5.6 times coarser. If the feature map is flattened without a scan-order design, tokens that are adjacent along the depth axis (D) are also adjacent in the sequence, and the relationship along D may be weighted more strongly than the in-plane structure.

Can anisotropy-aware scan ordering improve Mamba-based cardiac deformable registration?
